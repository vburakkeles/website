Paymint

Banking, payments and crypto — in one rhythm.

ResearchBrandingWireframingUX/UI DesignPrototypingiOSAndroid
Product overview

Paymint is a mobile finance app that brings everyday banking, instant transfers, QR payments and a crypto wallet into one place. Instead of switching between a bank app, a payment app and an exchange, users see one balance, one activity feed and one way to pay — with the same clarity whether they're sending lira to a friend or Bitcoin to an external wallet.

Problem statement

People who use both traditional money and crypto live in two or three apps with different mental models, different confirmation patterns and different levels of trust. Sending crypto feels risky, QR payments feel clumsy, and no single screen answers the basic question: how much can I actually spend right now?

Money should feel the same whether it's in your card, your wallet or your chain.
Goals
1

Unify fiat and crypto in one balance and one activity feed

2

Make every transfer reviewable before it's final — and instantly confirmable after

3

Turn QR payment into a three-second, no-thinking flow

4

Keep crypto transfers safe with explicit network and address checks

5

Build trust through a calm, consistent dark interface

Digitally native adults aged 22–40 who pay by phone daily and hold at least one crypto asset.

Design features
Feature 01

One balance, two worlds

A single Home switches between Balance and Crypto with the same layout, card and quick actions — so both feel like the same product.

Feature 02

Pay with what you have

QR payments can be settled from the lira balance or converted from Bitcoin on the spot, with the fee and equivalent shown before confirming.

Feature 03

Review before it's real

Every transfer — money or crypto — goes through a plain-language review screen and Face ID, then a receipt you can share.

Feature 04

Safe crypto sends

Network, address preview and an explicit 'can't be reversed' notice sit right above the confirm button, where they can't be missed.

Markets

Portfolio value, weekly change and available-to-trade in one header; assets carry their live change so the feed doubles as a watchlist.

Screen 3

One plain-language card — who, how much, fee, total — followed by a single Face ID-protected confirm. Nothing is final until this screen.

Merchant is verified, amount is read from the code, and the user simply chooses Balance or Crypto. Fees and equivalents update before paying.

Key takeaways
01

Same shape, different money

Balance and Crypto share a familiar layout to reduce the learning required when switching between them. The intended effect on trust should be evaluated in task-based testing.

02

Reassurance is a screen, not a tooltip

A dedicated review step places network, address and fee information before confirmation, with biometric authentication in the proposed flow. Its effect on abandonment requires measurement.

03

Speed is a feature of payment

The QR flow minimizes decisions between scanning and confirmation. Completion time and error rate are the measures needed to validate the intended speed advantage.
