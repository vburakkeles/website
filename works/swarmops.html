SwarmOps

Commanding multiple drone swarms and a live fire-response operation from a single tactical map.

ResearchBrandingWireframingUX/UI DesignPrototypingDesktop
Product overview

SwarmOps is an advanced tactical control panel where a single operator manages multiple drone swarms and independent drones on one map, plans missions dynamically, and directs fire-suppression UAVs. It was delivered as a 40-screen interactive prototype and a 21-screen scenario flow.

Problem statement

The operator has to command several swarms plus independent drones on one surface — creating a new swarm mid-crisis, handling a drone failure, responding to spreading fires, and returning every unit home safely — all without losing situational context or making a costly mis-click under pressure.

When one wrong tap can ground a fleet, the interface's job is to make the right move the obvious one.
Goals
1

Unify multi-swarm and independent-drone control on a single tactical map

2

Support dynamic mission planning — no-fly zones, re-routing, new swarms mid-mission

3

Prevent operator error with confirmation and alert systems on critical commands

4

Keep fast-changing telemetry legible with monospace type and fixed-layout panels

5

Enable fluid macro-to-micro movement, from fleet overview down to single-drone diagnostics

Design process
01

Reference analysis

Studied 12 products across ground-control software, military C2, RTS games and monitoring dashboards to collect proven patterns for high-pressure control.

02

Scenario definition

Wrote a 21-step fire-response scenario — new swarm, drone failure, spreading fire, return-to-base — as the backbone every screen had to serve.

03

Design

Wireframes to a 40-screen high-fidelity Figma prototype with a fixed layout, dynamic detail panel and consistent unit colours.

04

Scenario walkthrough

Validated the flow by walking the full scenario in the prototype; no field tests with operators were run at this stage.

Target audience
Primary

Drone-swarm operators and tactical command staff coordinating multi-unit emergency response in real time.

Secondary

Mission analysts and response coordinators monitoring fleet status and reviewing after-action reports.

Design features
Feature 01

One map, every unit

Swarms and independent drones share one tactical surface with consistent unit colours, so 'who is where' never needs a second screen.

Feature 02

Macro / micro toggle

Borrowed from RTS strategic views: zoom to a single drone while a mini-map keeps the fleet in sight.

Feature 03

Guarded critical commands

Redirect, return-to-base and emergency landing require confirmation and surface alerts before anything moves.

Feature 04

Dynamic mission planning

No-fly zones, re-routing and new swarm creation happen mid-mission without leaving the map.

Design implications

Patterns shared across at least three domains — side panel + map, confirmation on critical commands, colour-coded units — were adopted as the backbone; domain-specific patterns were checked against the 21-step scenario before being kept.

High fidelity screens
Screen 1

Ready state

System ready — ALPHA and BRAVO on standby, FIRE-01 detected. The left panel lists active units with capacity, battery, ammo and status; the right detail panel stays empty until something is selected.

Screen 2

Macro / micro & mini-map

The Macro/Micro toggle — borrowed from Supreme Commander's strategic view — zooms in to individual drones while a mini-map widget preserves the macro context.

Screen 3

Drone diagnostics

Micro drill-down to DRN-15: motor M2 running at 32% RPM, asymmetric thrust flagged. Three unambiguous actions — Return to base, Emergency landing, Continue mission — keep the critical decision clear.

Screen 4

Return-to-base routes

With the mission complete, all three units head home on separate colour-coded trajectories — ALPHA purple, BRAVO pink, CHARLIE orange — so 'who is going where' stays readable at a glance.

Key takeaways
01

Design the interface to prevent mistakes

A fixed layout with a dynamic right panel, confirmation on destructive commands, and consistent unit colours were selected to guard against error paths in the scenario walkthrough. Their effect on real operator safety remains a hypothesis for field validation.

02

Borrow from where pressure is real

Ground-control software, military C2 and RTS games had already solved most of the problems; the work was choosing and combining, not inventing.

03

Legibility supports safer decisions

Monospace telemetry in fixed-width panels stopped the layout shifting under fast-changing numbers — a small choice with a large effect.
